# Baseline prompt — Northstar

Build a complete, polished, responsive multi-page marketing website for Northstar, a fictional workflow planning SaaS for small operations teams. This is the BASELINE benchmark condition: use your normal product-design and coding approach. Do not use or reference PWDS, an external design-skill pack, or any special benchmark rubric.

## Product facts (only factual source)

Northstar helps operations teams coordinate recurring work, assign ownership, and see delivery risks. Audience: operations leads and team managers at companies with 10–200 people. Primary business goal: qualified visitors start a product trial. Secondary goal: visitors understand the workflow and pricing approach.

Do not invent integrations, certifications, customer adoption, quantified outcomes, testimonials, customer logos, ratings, press mentions, or security badges. Any sample UI data must be visibly labelled "Sample data". Prices must be labelled "Illustrative pricing".

## Required routes

1. Home: differentiated hero, product explanation, workflow, use cases, pricing teaser, FAQ, final CTA.
2. Pricing: at least two clearly differentiated plans with transparent placeholder prices labelled "Illustrative pricing".
3. Product: explain Plan → Assign → Monitor with an original, truthful product UI illustration. Any names, tasks, or numbers inside the illustration must be clearly marked sample data.

## Required interactions

- Responsive navigation and accessible mobile menu.
- Primary and secondary CTAs navigate to working destinations.
- Pricing toggle only if monthly/annual values are both clearly labelled and mathematically consistent; otherwise omit it.
- FAQ disclosures support keyboard interaction and appropriate semantics.
- Email capture form has client-side validation, pending, success, and error states. There is no real submission endpoint: clearly label this as a demo and do not claim an email was actually sent or stored.
- All interactive controls work with keyboard and touch.

## Design quality

Choose a distinctive premium-commercial art direction, not a generic SaaS template. Make deliberate choices for typography, color, spacing, composition, product visualization, imagery, and motion. Build a coherent reusable design system. Avoid default gradients, glassmorphism, floating blobs, arbitrary glow, and card grids unless justified by a coherent concept. Prioritize clear hierarchy and a believable product UI over decorative filler. Use original CSS/SVG illustrations or generated visual assets; do not hotlink unlicensed third-party assets.

## Responsive targets

Ensure intentional layouts at 360×800, 390×844, 768×1024, 1366×768, and 1440×900. No horizontal overflow, clipped essential content, or controls hidden off-screen. Recompose rather than merely shrink desktop layout.

## Engineering

Use the environment's default stack and established conventions. Prefer maintainable components, semantic HTML, route navigation, and minimal dependencies. No backend or real signup integration is required. Ensure no broken links, runtime errors, or console errors.

## Deliverable

Implement all three routes and interactions. At completion, summarize design decisions, files/routes built, actual checks performed, and anything not verified. Do not claim browser, accessibility, performance, or viewport checks unless you actually ran them.
