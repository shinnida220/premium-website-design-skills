# Conversion Design

Improve the user's ability and confidence to take a relevant next step. Conversion optimization must be truthful, respectful, and aligned with the product's actual value.

## Understand the journey
- Identify the audience, intent, awareness level, decision risk, and primary conversion.
- Map entry points, information needs, objections, proof requirements, action, and confirmation.
- Distinguish primary conversion from secondary exploration.
- Review existing analytics or research when available; do not invent performance data.

## Design workflow
1. State the value proposition in specific, audience-relevant language.
2. Make the next action visible and label it with an accurate verb.
3. Provide the information users need before asking for commitment.
4. Place evidence near the claims it supports; use only verified proof.
5. Reduce unnecessary form fields and explain why sensitive information is needed.
6. Make errors actionable, preserve user input, and provide clear success/failure feedback.
7. Set expectations about cost, timing, eligibility, privacy, and next steps.
8. Test the complete flow on mobile, keyboard, and assistive technology.

## Ethical constraints
- No fake countdowns, false scarcity, preselected consent, hidden fees, misleading button labels, or obstructive cancellation.
- Do not disguise ads or paid placement as neutral recommendations.
- Do not imply guaranteed outcomes without evidence.
- Respect privacy, consent, and data minimization.

## Measurement
Define an event or outcome that can actually be measured. Separate hypotheses from observed results. Recommend experiments only when traffic, instrumentation, and sample size can support meaningful conclusions.

## Deliverable
Provide journey map, primary/secondary CTA hierarchy, key information and proof requirements, form/interaction recommendations, measurement plan, accessibility considerations, and risks.
