# Brand Art Direction

Turn business and audience context into a specific creative direction that can guide design decisions consistently.

## Inputs

Use available project information first: product brief, brand assets, existing site, audience, competitors, content, and business goals. Identify unknowns that could change the direction; ask only essential questions.

## Process

1. **Define the brand problem.** What should visitors understand, feel, trust, and do?
2. **Identify audience context.** Consider expertise, urgency, risk, familiarity, device, and accessibility needs.
3. **Set positioning.** Describe the product's differentiator in plain language. Do not invent claims.
4. **Choose a visual concept.** Express it as a memorable design idea, not a list of trendy effects.
5. **Set visual behavior.** Specify typography character, color logic, imagery subject/treatment, layout energy, surface language, icon approach, and motion character.
6. **Define boundaries.** Name what the design should avoid and why.
7. **Connect concept to execution.** Explain how the direction affects hero, navigation, content sections, proof, CTA, and responsive behavior.

## Direction quality

A useful direction is:
- Specific enough that two pages feel part of the same brand.
- Appropriate to audience trust expectations and product category.
- Distinct from generic SaaS/agency templates.
- Executable with available content and assets.
- Flexible across screen sizes and accessibility needs.

Avoid moodboard-only language such as “modern, clean, sleek” unless translated into observable design decisions. Avoid copying a reference site's exact composition, identity, or assets.

## Deliverable

Return:
- Brand/design objective.
- Audience and positioning summary.
- One-sentence creative concept.
- Visual principles (3–6).
- Typography, color, imagery, composition, surface, and motion direction.
- Anti-goals and constraints.
- How the direction appears on key page sections.

If several directions are viable, present at most three genuinely distinct options and recommend one with reasons.
